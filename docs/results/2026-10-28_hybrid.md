# Hybrid pipeline, phase 1 (2026-10-28)

Read (Muse Spark transcribes the schematic image), Model (fault catalog generated from the transcribed
netlist), Plan (information-gain planner on the TRANSCRIBED netlist, readings from the TRUE circuit),
Verify (a reading no candidate explains stops the planner; Muse Spark proposes a netlist correction,
the planner rebuilds and replays every reading), Explain (Muse Spark writes a technician-facing
explanation). Code: `src/probeforge/hybrid/`, planner `src/probeforge/baseline/infogain.py`.

Evaluation input: transcription run 2 of test B (`scenarios/transcriptions/star_delta_run2.yaml`, a real
Muse Spark output, unedited): 6 of 35 nets wrong (the KM2 reversing crossover and the KM4/KM3 delta
links), the motor reads "stopped" in delta, and no timer delay, trip time or rated current (the drawing
does not print them). Clean holdout, 43 faults, approval requests denied, repair at reasoning effort
medium, explanation at low. Model calls never see the true netlist or the injected fault; each
correction is compared with the true netlist after the answer, for the log only.

Every miss is an honest insufficient_information, never a wrong component.

## Verify step

- Readings that contradicted every candidate: 33, in 31 of 43 cases (the same 31 cases where the
  no-repair run hands over).
- Corrections proposed by Muse Spark: 35 (no unusable answer, no retry needed).
- Resolved the conflict on replay (some candidate fits every reading again): 32 of 35.
- Healthy operating sequence right after the correction (motor reaches delta and reverse like the
  real machine): 29 of 35.
- Netlist exactly equal to the true one after a correction: 0 of 35. The typical correction rewires
  the KM4 delta links to the true KM4 pins (the delta now works), but keeps the transcription's KM3
  star-side pin order and the KM2 crossover variant, which are permutations a healthy panel cannot
  show. Example (c14):

  ```
  - net U2: [KM3:1, KM4:2, M1:U2]
  - net V2: [KM3:3, KM4:4, M1:V2]
  - net W2: [KM3:5, KM4:6, M1:W2]
  + net U2: [KM3:1, KM4:4, M1:U2]
  + net V2: [KM3:3, KM4:6, M1:V2]
  + net W2: [KM3:5, KM4:2, M1:W2]
  ```

  True: U2 = [KM3:3, KM4:4, M1:U2], V2 = [KM3:5, KM4:6, M1:V2], W2 = [KM3:1, KM4:2, M1:W2].
- Wrong corrections are caught: in c15 Muse Spark tied a motor terminal into the wrong net and once
  changed the supply voltage to 133 V; replay found no consistent candidate three times and the planner
  handed over to a human (the only handover). No wrong correction led to a wrong diagnosis.
- Explanations citing at least one step: 40 of 43.

So, counted strictly, the netlist was never fully repaired; counted by what matters for the diagnosis,
32 of 35 corrections made the model consistent with the readings, and 29 of 35 made the healthy machine
behave correctly.

## Why the remaining misses

The 6 visible misses that are not observational twins (c05, c07, c08, c15, c18, c28) are all KM1 / KM2 /
KM4 main poles. The leftover pin permutations (which pole feeds which winding) are exactly what tells
these poles apart, and because the drawing gives no motor rating, the planner compares line currents only
as zero / nominal / overcurrent, so it cannot use per-line amps either. It ends with 2 or 3 equally
possible poles and answers insufficient_information. The 3 justified misses are the known twins.

## Design decisions made during the build

- **Unprinted values are unknowns, not defaults.** With run 2 the simulator would assume a 0 s timer
  delay. An offline dry run showed that this makes the planner diagnose KM4:3-4 stuck_open as "KT stuck"
  with full confidence, without any conflict. Now a missing timer delay is a candidate dimension
  (1, 3, 10, 30 s), as is a missing trip time (3, 10, 30 s); the grids are log-spaced and do not contain
  the true values (5 s, 8 s). Without a rated current, currents are compared by category only.
- **Candidates whose simulation never settles are dropped** (found with a live transcription in the UI,
  where some faults made the transcribed relays chatter and stopped the run).
- **Probe points are terminal ids**, which the drawing and the real panel share; net names are the
  model's own.
- The planner refactor is behaviour-identical for the information-gain baseline: same diagnosis and
  measurement count on all 43 clean and 8 scenario cases.

## Read step: uncertain regions (new transcriptions with flags)

`scripts/transcribe_schematic.py` (now asking for an `uncertain` list), 3 runs, effort medium:

| Run | Nets exact | Flags | Flags touching a wrong net | Wrong nets inside a flagged region | $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 28/35 | 9 | 4 | 7/7 | 0.081 |
| 2 | 29/35 | 6 | 2 | 6/6 | 0.060 |

Every wrong net was inside a flagged region in all three runs; about half of the flags are false alarms
(regions read correctly). In the UI the flagged terminals light up on the schematic and the run waits
for a human to confirm; batch runs accept the transcription as it is.
